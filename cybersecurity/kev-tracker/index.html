S

Situation

Tens of thousands of new CVEs are published every year, far more than any team can patch. CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog lists the ones confirmed to be exploited in the wild, which makes it one of the most practical signals for deciding what to fix first. But the raw data is hard to read for trends.

T

Task

Turn the KEV catalog into an interactive dashboard that answers vulnerability-management questions: which vendors and weakness types dominate, how quickly flaws get exploited, and how much is linked to ransomware. The project combines my data science and security skills.

A

Action

  • Built an R Shiny app with Overview, Insights, Explore and About pages, and filters for date, vendor, ransomware use and free-text search that apply across every page.
  • Derived new measures: vulnerability age when it was added (year added minus CVE year) and the patch window (due date minus date added).
  • Added key findings that recalculate for any filter, a vendor-by-year heatmap, a searchable table linked to the NVD, and CSV export.
  • Made the app download CISA's live feed at start-up, falling back to a bundled snapshot, and used a colour-blind-safe palette.
R

Result

  • Of 1,766 entries (to 30 Oct 2026), Microsoft accounts for 22%, with Windows alone at 175.
  • 45% were exploited in the same year their CVE was assigned, while 16% were more than 5 years old when added. Attackers move fast, and old unpatched systems stay targets.
  • 20% are linked to ransomware campaigns, and the most common patch deadline is 21 days.
  • The findings shaped my detection rules. For example, path traversal (CWE-22), one of the most common KEV weaknesses, got its own rule.
